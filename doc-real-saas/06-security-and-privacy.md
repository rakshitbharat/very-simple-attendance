# Trust and Privacy Principles

This document describes the customer promise. It intentionally does not prescribe technology.

## Trust promise

Employees should trust that their attendance is accurate and cannot be changed silently. Owners should trust that records belong to the correct company, actions are attributable, and information remains available when needed.

## Product principles

- People see only the companies and records they are permitted to see.
- Employees can see their own attendance and correction history.
- Personal Google-account access information is never visible to company administrators; authorized people see only what is needed to approve or revoke a company device.
- Important changes identify who changed what, when, and why.
- The product clearly distinguishes a confirmed action from an offline, pending, or failed action.
- The product explains that PTP grants company attendance permission to a device; it is not a replacement for personal Google login.

## Privacy principles

- Collect the minimum information needed to operate attendance and subscriptions.
- Explain what the company can see about an employee.
- Let customers export their information in a usable format.
- Define retention and deletion before accepting real employee data.
- Avoid location, biometrics, screenshots, or continuous surveillance unless a separately validated need justifies the privacy cost.
- Do not sell employee attendance data or use it for unrelated profiling.

## Fairness and transparency

- Never modify an attendance record invisibly.
- Preserve the original value and the correction explanation.
- Avoid automatically labelling someone absent when the product lacks a schedule or leave information.
- Make time zone and calculation rules visible in reports.
- Describe calculated totals as recorded attendance unless a stronger business process supports another claim.

## Customer controls

- Owners can manage who belongs to their organization.
- People can recover access without learning another person’s secret.
- A departing company can export its records.
- Organization deletion is deliberate, explained, and consistent with the retention promise.
- A person can understand where to raise an access, privacy, or correction concern.

## Policy documents needed before a paid pilot

- Privacy notice
- Terms of service
- Data retention and deletion policy
- Attendance-data ownership/responsibility statement
- Support and incident contact
- Subscription cancellation and refund policy

The exact legal obligations depend on the launch market and require appropriate professional review.

## Open trust questions

- Which attendance disputes are most common in the target segment?
- Should device approval require the approver and member to be together, or can it safely happen remotely?
- What is the safe recovery path when all approved devices are lost?
- Who is allowed to correct owner/manager attendance?
- How long do businesses need historical records?
- What should an employee retain access to after leaving?
- Which events should generate notifications to employees?
