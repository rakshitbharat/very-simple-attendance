# Business and Subscription Hypotheses

All pricing in this document is a test design, not a final price list. Currency and tax handling depend on the first market.

## Commercial thesis

Tiny businesses will pay a small recurring amount for attendance when the product saves monthly reconciliation time, reduces disputes, and is materially simpler than an HR suite.

## Recommended packaging experiment

Keep packaging understandable in one glance:

- **Trial:** full core experience for a short fixed period; no card requirement is an experiment.
- **Starter:** up to a small included member count, core attendance, corrections, and CSV export.
- **Team:** up to 20 active members, manager permissions, richer reports, and priority support.

Alternative experiment: one base organization fee plus a small price per active member, capped at 20. Compare comprehension and revenue quality rather than assuming one structure wins.

## Avoid at first

- A permanently free multi-user tier that creates support cost without a conversion path
- Charging for archived members or pending invites
- Feature matrices with many artificial restrictions
- Annual-only commitments before trust is established
- Usage metrics customers cannot independently understand

## Entitlement rules

- The product shows one consistent plan and active-member count.
- Repeated or delayed payment updates never create duplicate charges or contradictory access.
- Payment failure receives a communicated grace period.
- Read/export access should remain available during a reasonable restricted state.
- Cancellation takes effect at period end unless refund policy says otherwise.
- An owner can see exactly which memberships count toward the limit.

## Billing states to design

Trial, active, payment problem, grace period, restricted, canceled, and incomplete are useful customer-facing concepts. Their language and consequences must be understandable without knowledge of a payment service.

## Validation interviews

Interview 10–15 owners/operators before locking scope. Ask about the last time attendance caused work or conflict, current process, monthly effort, who corrects records, device context, reports needed, and what they already pay. Avoid leading with the proposed solution or price.

## Pricing test

After problem interviews, show a clickable workflow and ask for a concrete next step: paid pilot, refundable deposit, or scheduled onboarding. “Would you pay?” is weak evidence; an actual commitment is strong evidence.

## Unit economics to track

- Monthly recurring revenue
- Trial-to-paid conversion
- Revenue and support time per organization
- Payment and service-delivery cost
- Gross margin
- Logo and revenue churn
- Customer acquisition cost and payback period, once acquisition becomes repeatable

## Support model

Start with in-product help, a short setup checklist, clear error messages, and email support. Capture every support request by category; recurring questions should improve the product or documentation before hiring around them.

## Go-to-market hypothesis

Begin with one narrow segment and geography where the team can personally onboard customers. Founder-led onboarding provides the fastest feedback on policy, trust, pricing, and missing workflows.
